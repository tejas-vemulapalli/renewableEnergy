Renewable energy is energy harvested from natural sources that can constantly be replenished within a human lifespan. There are many alternatives to fossil fuels — solar, wind, geothermal, and hydro-electric — and these are called renewable energy. Using these could deeply help climate change.

It is our responsibility to adopt renewable energy sources.

---

## What renewable energy is

There are four main types of renewable energy:

- Solar
- Wind
- Hydro
- Geothermal

These four types rely on light, kinetic energy, and heat.

```
Renewable energy
(replenished within a human lifespan)
│
├── Solar
├── Wind
├── Hydro        ← most efficient of the four
│                 converts 90% of kinetic energy into usable power
└── Geothermal
```

Out of the four sources, hydropower is the most efficient, converting 90% of kinetic energy into usable power.

## How the world uses energy today

The most commonly used sources of energy are fossil fuels, which burn carbon-based materials, boosting greenhouse gas emissions and climate change.

About 40% of the world's energy comes from renewable sources:

- **5%** is solar
- **10%** is wind
- **17%** is hydro

This shows how effective renewable energy can be at maintaining a clean and sustainable environment.

---

## Fossil fuels and climate change

Fossil fuels are the main contributor to climate change.

Burning them releases greenhouse gases, primarily carbon dioxide, which trap heat in the atmosphere. This rising global temperature melts polar ice caps and increases sea levels. Additionally, the smoke emissions fill the air with harmful air pollutants and soot, which degrade air quality and further accelerate warming.

Using fossil fuels is bad for the environment because:

- Mining and drilling for coal destroy landscapes and ecosystems, endangering animals.
- Burning coal releases carbon dioxide which gets trapped in the atmosphere, creating a heat blanket around the earth.

```
Burning fossil fuels
        │
        ▼
Greenhouse gases (primarily carbon dioxide)
        │
        ▼
Heat trapped in the atmosphere
        │
        ├── Polar ice caps melt
        ├── Sea levels increase
        └── Smoke: harmful air pollutants and soot
              → poorer air quality
              → further warming
```

## Global impacts of fossil fuels

The use of fossil fuels impacts many parts of the world, but the most affected zone is Asia.

- This area consumes **60%** of the world's coal.
- Countries including South Korea and Japan heavily rely on imported coal.
- **20%** of the world's coal comes through the Strait of Hormuz, which is currently closed due to the recent conflict between Iran and the USA.
- This forced countries to delay shutdown of coal fired plants and max the input on coal fired plants.

## Australia's renewable energy goal

The Australian Government has a goal for renewable energy: to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by **42% by 2030**.

They plan to do this by converting things such as transportation and large scale buildings to more energy efficient electrical models. This will also improve energy performance.

In addition to this, adding more renewable implants across the country can generate new jobs, such as maintenance and installation, which can help stabilise economic structures. This will help the government adopt independence and save money for future funding.

The government can help this process by:

- lowering the prices of solar panels
- providing rebates for grid generation to people who install solar panels on their home or business

## Downsides of renewable energy when mass produced

Renewable energy still comes with downsides if mass produced.

| Source | Downside |
| --- | --- |
| Solar panels | Affect desert biomes by clearing land and disturbing desert water balance |
| Wind turbines in forests | Can also clear land and block migration paths |
| Hydropower with dams | Can affect natural water flow and block migration paths |
| Geothermal (rare circumstances) | Can lead to acid rain by releasing hydrogen atoms into a moist atmosphere |
