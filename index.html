Overview of Renewable Energy

There are four main types of renewable energy: solar, wind, hydro, and geothermal. These rely on light, kinetic energy, and heat.

Hydropower is the most efficient, converting 90% of kinetic energy into usable power.

40% of the world's energy comes from renewable sources: 5% solar, 10% wind, and 17% hydro.

Fossil Fuels & Climate Change

Fossil fuels burn carbon-based materials, increasing greenhouse gases and accelerating climate change.

Mining and drilling destroy landscapes and ecosystems. Burning coal releases CO₂, creating a heat blanket around Earth.

Rising temperatures melt polar ice caps, raise sea levels, and worsen air pollution.

Global Coal Use

Asia consumes 60% of the world's coal. Countries like South Korea and Japan rely heavily on imported coal.

20% of the world's coal passes through the Strait of Hormuz, currently closed due to conflict, forcing countries to delay shutdowns of coal-fired plants.

Australian Government Goals

Australia aims to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 42% by 2030.

They plan to convert transportation and large buildings to more energy-efficient electrical models.

Renewable expansion creates jobs in maintenance and installation, stabilizing economic structures.

Downsides of Renewable Energy

Solar panels can disturb desert biomes. Wind turbines can block migration paths. Hydropower dams affect water flow. Geothermal energy can rarely cause acid rain.

Alternatives & Everyday Actions

Renewable energy includes solar, wind, geothermal, and hydro-electric sources.

If you cannot afford renewable solutions, simple actions like turning off lights or heaters help reduce energy use.
