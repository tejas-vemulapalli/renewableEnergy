What is Renewable Energy?

Renewable energy comes from natural sources that can be replenished within a human lifespan. These include solar, wind, hydro, and geothermal energy.

40% of the world’s energy comes from renewable sources: 5% solar, 10% wind, and 17% hydro.

Why We Need Renewable Energy

Fossil fuels burn carbon-based materials, releasing greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. This accelerates climate change, melts polar ice caps, raises sea levels, and worsens air quality.

Mining and drilling for coal destroy landscapes and ecosystems, endangering wildlife.

Types of Renewable Energy

Solar

Uses sunlight to generate power.

Wind

Uses kinetic energy from wind.

Hydropower

The most efficient renewable source, converting 90% of kinetic energy.

Geothermal

Uses heat from the Earth. Rarely can cause acid rain in moist air.

Global Coal Use

Asia consumes 60% of the world’s coal. South Korea and Japan rely heavily on imported coal. 20% of global coal passes through the Strait of Hormuz, currently closed due to conflict.

Australian Government Goals

Australia aims to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 42% by 2030. Plans include converting transportation and large buildings to efficient electrical models and expanding renewable energy jobs.

How Natural Sources Become Usable Energy

This simplified process shows how renewable sources are converted into usable electricity:

1. Capture

Sunlight, wind, water flow, or geothermal heat is collected.

2. Conversion

Solar panels, turbines, or heat exchangers convert natural energy into mechanical or electrical energy.

3. Transmission

Electricity is sent through power lines to homes and businesses.

4. Use

Energy powers appliances, lighting, heating, and industry.
